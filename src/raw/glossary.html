Glossary — A to Z

Every term a photographer meets, one line each, gathered from every page of this shelf — genres, technique, gear, history, and the business of shooting. For the longer definitions, grouped by topic, read the terminology page.

One line each, quick-scan style — a term to look up, not a lesson to read. Deeper water waits on the page each term belongs to.

A

Aberration
An optical flaw — color fringing or warped lines; every lens has some.
AE lock
Holds a metered exposure while you recompose.
AF
Autofocus — the system that drives the lens until the chosen point is sharp.
Aliasing
Stair-stepped false patterns from undersampled detail; moiré is its visible child.
Ambient light
The light already in the scene — sun, lamps, sky — not added by you.
Angle of view
How much of the scene the lens takes in, measured in degrees.
Aperture
The adjustable opening that meters light through the lens.
Aperture priority
A/Av: you pick the f-number, the camera picks the shutter.
APS-C
The roughly 1.5×-crop sensor size in most entry and mid-range bodies.
Aspect ratio
Width to height of the frame — 3:2, 4:3, 1:1, 16:9.
Auto ISO
The camera picks sensitivity within limits you set; the quiet workhorse.

B

Back-button focus
Moving autofocus to a rear button, separating focus from the shutter.
Backlight
Light from behind the subject toward the camera; silhouettes and rim glow.
Barrel distortion
Straight lines bowing outward — the wide-angle signature.
Base ISO
The sensor's cleanest, least-amplified setting, usually 100 or 64.
Black-and-white
Images of tone without hue; the medium's oldest look.
Bit depth
Tonal steps per channel — 8-bit holds 256, 16-bit vastly more headroom.
Bokeh
How out-of-focus areas look — smooth or busy — not how much blur. See aperture.
Bracketing
Shooting several exposures of one frame, to hedge or to blend.
Burst mode
Holding the shutter fires a run of frames; the sports safety net.

C

Camera obscura
The darkened room or box that projected images centuries before film.
Camera shake
Blur from the camera moving during exposure, not the subject.
Catchlight
The bright reflection of a light source in a subject's eye.
Chimping
Staring at the rear screen after every shot instead of at the scene.
Chromatic aberration
Colored fringes where a lens focuses wavelengths unequally.
Client gallery
A private online space for proofs, picks, and delivery.
Clipping
Tones pushed to pure black or white, detail unrecoverable.
CMOS
The sensor design in nearly every modern camera.
Color grading
Deliberate stylization of color across shadows, midtones, highlights.
Color temperature
The warmth or coolness of light, measured in kelvin.
Contact sheet
A roll printed small on one sheet; the editor's first pass.
Contrast
The spread between dark and light — the curve controls it.
Crop factor
How much a smaller sensor narrows a lens's view — 1.5×, 2×.
Cropping
Trimming the frame after capture to recompose or fit a ratio.

D

Daguerreotype
The first public photographic process, 1839 — a unique image on silvered plate.
Darkroom
The red-lit room where film became negative and negative became print.
Demosaic
Computing full color from a sensor's one-color-per-pixel filter array.
Depth of field
The zone that looks acceptably sharp around the focus point.
Diaphragm
The iris of overlapping blades that forms the aperture.
Diffraction
Softening from light squeezing through a tiny opening — the tax past f/11 or so.
Dodging
Hand-lightening part of a print in the darkroom; a local tool today.
DNG
Adobe's open RAW container, meant to outlive proprietary formats.
DPI
Dots per inch — a printer spec; screens deal in pixels.
Dynamic range
The span of tones from darkest to brightest with detail, in stops.

E

Emulsion
The light-sensitive coating on film or paper.
Environmental portrait
A portrait in the subject's own space, context in frame.
EV
Exposure value — a log scale where each step is one stop.
EXIF
The metadata inside every file: camera, lens, settings, time.
Exposure
The total light recorded — intensity × time × sensitivity.
Exposure compensation
Deliberate ± override of the camera's automatic exposure.
Exposure triangle
Aperture, shutter, ISO — three ways to spend the same light.
Export
Rendering a copy at a chosen size, format, and color space.
Extension tube
A hollow ring that lets a lens focus closer — cheap macro.

F

f-number
Focal length divided by aperture diameter; smaller means more light.
Fast lens
Slang for a wide maximum aperture — f/1.4 to f/2.8 — not focusing speed.
Fill light
The dimmer, softer source that lifts shadows the key casts.
Fixer
Darkroom chemistry that makes an image permanent.
Fisheye
An ultra-wide lens that keeps its extreme angle by letting lines bend.
Flare
Stray light scattered inside the lens — veils and ghost spots, sometimes kept.
Focal length
The lens's magnification figure in millimeters; sets angle of view.
Focal plane
The surface where the lens draws its sharp image — where the sensor sits.
Focus peaking
Colored outlines marking in-focus edges; a manual-focus aid.
Full frame
A sensor the size of a 35mm film frame — 36×24 mm.

G

Gamut
The full range of colors a device or color space can represent.
Gel
Colored film over a light to change its color or correct it.
Golden hour
The low, warm light of the first and last hour of sun — see natural light.
Grain
The clumping of developed silver in film; noise's charming ancestor.
Gray card
A neutral reference used to meter or set white balance precisely.
Guide number
A flash-power figure: GN divided by distance gives the f-number.

H

Hard light
From a small or distant source — crisp-edged shadows, high contrast.
HDR
High dynamic range: blending bracketed frames into one wide-toned image.
Highlight
The brightest tones in a frame, guarded against clipping.
High key
A bright image built mostly of light tones; airy, minimal shadow.
Histogram
The bar chart of a photo's tones from black to white — see the histogram.
Hot shoe
The top bracket that mounts and triggers a flash.
HSS
High-speed sync: flash beyond normal sync speed, paid for in power.
Hyperfocal distance
The focus distance that keeps half of it to infinity acceptably sharp.

I

IBIS
In-body image stabilization — the sensor floats against camera shake.
Incident light
Light falling on the subject, measured from the subject toward the camera.
Infinity
The farthest focus distance — distant subjects at ∞.
Intervalometer
A timer that fires frames automatically, for timelapses and star trails.
Inverse square law
Light's intensity falls with the square of distance — why near lights are soft.
IS / VR / OS
Image stabilization by brand — Canon, Nikon, Sigma; same idea.
ISO
The sensitivity setting, inherited from film speed ratings.

J

JPEG
The universal compressed photo format — convenient, 8-bit, lossy.
JPEG artifact
Blockiness and halos left by heavy compression.

K

Kelvin
The color-temperature scale — candle about 1,800 K, daylight about 5,500 K.
Key light
The main light, setting the pattern and the exposure.
Kit lens
The modest zoom sold with a body; better than its reputation.

L

Landscape
The genre of land, weather, and light over long patience.
Latitude
How far an exposure can stray and still be pulled back — RAW has plenty.
Leading lines
Frame edges that steer the eye to the subject — see leading lines.
License
Permission to use an image, with scope and term — the heart of photo contracts.
Live view
Composing on the rear screen, sensor feeding it directly.
Long exposure
Seconds-long shutters for motion, smoothing, and dim light.
Low key
A dark image built mostly of shadow tones; moody, selective light.
Luminance
Brightness independent of color — the channel noise reduction prefers to touch.

M

Macro
Photography at or near life-size magnification on the sensor.
Manual mode
M: nothing changes unless you change it.
Matrix metering
The camera reading the whole frame against a library of scenes.
Megapixel
A million photosites — marketing's favorite integer.
Mirrorless
Cameras without the reflex mirror; the viewfinder is a screen.
Model release
Signed permission to publish someone's likeness commercially.
Moiré
Wavy interference patterns on fine repeating detail like fabric.
Monochrome
Single-channel rendering — black-and-white, plus toning.
MTF
A lens chart of contrast and resolution; skim it, don't worship it.

N

ND filter
Neutral gray glass cutting all light evenly — slow shutters in sunlight.
Negative
Film with tones reversed, printed to become positive.
Nifty fifty
The affectionate name for a 50mm f/1.8.
Noise
Random grain-like speckle, mostly in shadows at high ISO.
Night photography
The genre after dark — tripods, long shutters, cities and stars.

O

Optical viewfinder
Composing through glass and mirror, seeing the scene itself.
Overexposure
Too much light — highlights clip to white with no detail.

P

Panorama
A wide image stitched from several frames, or cropped to feel like one.
Panning
Tracking a moving subject at a slow shutter — sharp subject, streaked world.
Perspective
How size and position build depth in the frame.
Phase detection
Fast autofocus that measures direction and distance at once.
Pictorialism
The early movement that made photographs look like paintings.
Pixel
One photosite's color cell — the atom of the digital image.
Polarizing filter
Glass that cuts reflections and darkens skies at one rotation.
Portfolio
The curated dozen-plus that says what you do — see portfolios.
Portrait
The genre of one person, one frame, light that flatters.
Prime
A fixed-focal-length lens — faster and sharper per dollar than zooms.
Print
The physical end of the pipeline — ink or silver on paper.

Q

Quality of light
The catch-all judgment: how hard or soft, warm or cool, directional or flat.

R

RAW
The sensor's near-unprocessed data — the digital negative; see RAW.
Reciprocity failure
Film's habit of needing extra exposure at very long shutter times.
Resolution
Pixel count, or rendering fineness — more pixels, not necessarily sharper.
RGB
Red, green, blue — how screens mix color.
Rim light
Light from behind that draws a bright edge around the subject.
Royalty-free
Pay once, use within the license's terms — not the same as free.
Rule of thirds
Dividing the frame in thirds, subjects at the intersections — see thirds.

S

Saturation
The intensity of color, from gray to full strength.
Sensor
The chip converting light to numbers; size shapes noise and depth of field.
Sharpness
Perceived edge clarity — acutance and resolution served by contrast.
Shutter
The mechanism timing how long light reaches the sensor.
Shutter priority
S/Tv: you pick the shutter, the camera picks the aperture.
SLR
Single-lens reflex — one lens, a mirror, a prism viewfinder.
Soft light
From large, close sources — shadows feather away.
Softbox
A tent-like modifier turning a small flash into a big soft source.
Split toning
Tinting shadows and highlights toward different hues.
Stock
Images licensed repeatedly from a catalog — a volume game.
Stop
A doubling or halving of light — the exchange rate of exposure.
Street photography
Unposed life in public space — the cheapest genre, the hardest to master.
Sync speed
The fastest shutter that works with flash — the curtain's limit.

T

Teleconverter
An extra optical group that multiplies focal length, paid for in light.
Telephoto
Long lenses that magnify — 85mm portraits, 600mm birds.
TIFF
A lossless format print labs prefer — large and 16-bit capable.
TLR
Twin-lens reflex — one lens to view, one to take, waist-level.
Tone curve
The graph mapping input brightness to output — contrast's steering wheel.
Tripod
Three legs and a head — the sharpest purchase per dollar.
TTL
Through-the-lens flash metering — the camera meters the flash's test pulse.
Tungsten
Warm incandescent light, about 2,700 K — the indoor white-balance preset.

U

Umbrella
The cheapest modifier — bounce or shoot-through, folds like its namesake.
Underexposure
Too little light — shadows block, noise grows when lifted.
Unsharp mask
Sharpening by indirection: blur a copy, compare edges, boost them.
UV filter
Clear glass kept on lenses — modern value mostly as sacrificial insurance.

V

Vignetting
Corners darkening — a defect to correct or a look to keep.
Vibrance
Saturation with a conscience — boosts muted colors, protects skin.
Viewfinder
The window you compose through, optical or electronic.
Visual weight
What draws the eye — light, contrast, color, faces.

W

Wedding photography
Documentary pace with formal craft — one take, no retakes.
White balance
The setting that renders the light's color as neutral — see white balance.
Wide angle
Short lenses that take in much — 16 to 35mm; space and context.
Wildlife photography
Long lenses, longer waits, fieldcraft first.
Workflow
The repeatable path from card to delivered file — see workflow.

X

X-sync
The shutter speed at which flash and focal-plane curtain cooperate.

Y

Yellow filter
The classic black-and-white filter — darkens skies, keeps skin honest.

Z

Zone focusing
Preset distance and aperture; the sharp zone covers the action.
Zone System
Adams and Archer's mapping of scene tones to print tones, in ten zones — see the masters.
Zoom burst
Twisting the zoom during exposure for radial streaks.
Zoom lens
One lens covering a range of focal lengths.
