Terminology — the vocabulary of the craft

Every field builds a wall of words, and photography built its twice — once for the physics and once for the abbreviations. This page takes the wall down brick by brick: grouped vocabulary with short definitions, the words you will meet in manuals, reviews, and the rest of this library. One-line quick scans live in the A–Z glossary.

Exposure & the camera's body

The words that describe how much light a photograph received, and the machine that received it. Japanese calls the photograph itself 写真 — shashin, "a true copy" — and exposure 露出, roshutsu.

Exposure terms the grammar of light received

Exposure 露出
The total light recorded: intensity (aperture) × time (shutter) × amplification (ISO).
Stop
A doubling or halving of light — the exchange rate between the three exposure controls.
EV (exposure value)
A log scale where each step is one stop; EV 0 is a one-second exposure at f/1, ISO 100.
Overexposure
Too much light: highlights clip to pure white with no detail left to recover.
Underexposure
Too little light: shadows block to black, and lifting them later raises noise.
Highlight
The brightest tones of a frame — the zone the whites slider and the histogram's right wall police.
Shadow
The darkest tones that still carry detail — darker than that is blocked, not shadowed.
Midtone
The middle values the eye reads most; where local contrast and most of a photo's story live.
Clipping 白飛び・黒潰れ
Tones pushed past the file's limits into featureless white or black — Japanese says "flying white" and "collapsed black."
Dynamic range
The span from darkest to brightest recordable detail, counted in stops; sensors hold ten to fifteen.
Histogram
A bar chart of every tone in the frame, black to white — the exposure's X-ray. See the histogram.
Bracketing
Shooting the same frame at several exposures to hedge the guess or blend into HDR later.
Exposure compensation
The ± override that makes the camera's automatic guess brighter or darker than it intended.
Metering
How the camera measures scene brightness to choose its guess — matrix, center-weighted, or spot.
ETTR
"Expose to the right": brighten until just short of clipping, pull down later — cleaner shadows.
Sunny 16
The daylight rule: f/16 with a shutter of one over the ISO is a full exposure in bright sun.

Camera parts what the machine is made of

Body
The camera without a lens: sensor, shutter, processor, screens, and controls.
Sensor
The light-recording chip; its size shapes noise, depth of field, and what a "normal" lens is.
Shutter
The mechanical curtain — or electronic scan — that times how long light reaches the sensor.
Aperture / diaphragm 絞り
The adjustable iris of overlapping blades in the lens, sized in f-numbers. See aperture.
Viewfinder
The window you compose through — optical (through the lens via mirror) or electronic (a tiny screen).
Hot shoe
The top bracket that mounts a flash and talks to it — power and timing over sprung contacts.
Mirror box
The SLR chamber holding the hinged mirror that bounces light up to the viewfinder between shots.
IBIS
In-body image stabilization: the sensor itself floats on magnets to counter camera shake.
Mode dial
The top dial choosing P, A, S, and M — the four modes that matter. See modes.
Command dials
The thumb and forefinger wheels that change aperture, shutter, ISO, and compensation.
Live view
Composing on the rear screen with the sensor feeding it directly; how mirrorless always works.

Glass & focus

The words on a lens's barrel and in its reviews. The hardware itself is surveyed at gear & optics; here we only fix what the words mean.

Lens terms focal length and its consequences

Focal length
The lens's magnification figure in millimeters; it sets both magnification and angle of view.
F-number (f-stop)
Focal length divided by aperture diameter — smaller number, bigger hole, more light.
Bokeh ボケ
How out-of-focus areas look — smooth or busy — not how much of them there is.
Depth of field 被写界深度
The zone that looks acceptably sharp around the focus point; a third before, two thirds behind.
Hyperfocal distance
The focus distance that keeps everything from half of it to infinity acceptably sharp.
Minimum focus distance
How close the lens can get and still focus; it sets the lens's maximum magnification.
Crop factor
How much a smaller sensor narrows a given lens's view — 1.5×, 1.6×, or 2× versus full frame.
Equivalence
Comparing formats honestly: matching angle of view, depth of field, and noise, not just millimeters.
MTF
A lens lab chart of contrast and resolution at fine detail; skim it, don't worship it.
Fast / slow lens
Slang for maximum aperture — f/1.4 is fast, f/5.6 slow. Nothing to do with focusing speed.
Prime
A lens of one fixed focal length; usually faster, smaller, and sharper per dollar.
Zoom
A lens covering a focal-length range; flexibility bought with size, cost, and aperture.

Focus terms sharpness is a decision

Autofocus ピント
The system that drives the lens until the chosen point is sharp — pinto, the "point," in Japanese.
AF-S / AF-C
Single: focus once, for still subjects. Continuous: track the moving ones frame after frame.
Phase detection
Fast autofocus that reads direction and distance in one measurement; lives in SLRs and on-sensor pixels.
Contrast detection
Slower but precise autofocus that hunts by maximizing contrast; the live-view method.
Focus points
The selectable boxes in the frame where autofocus can lock — hundreds now, but the center ones still aim best.
Back-button focus
Moving autofocus start to a rear button, separating focusing from firing the shutter.
Focus peaking
Colored outlines over in-focus edges — the manual-focus aid that mirrorless and video live on.
Zone focusing
Preset distance and aperture so the sharp zone covers where the action will be; street's quiet trick. See street.
Focus breathing
The slight change of framing a lens makes while focusing — invisible in stills, obvious in video.
Focus hunting
Autofocus sliding back and forth without locking, usually on low-contrast or close-up subjects.

Light & color

Light is the medium — 光, hikari — and color is light read by the mind. The setups and the physics of each word below are worked through at light & lighting.

Light terms the names of the lights

Key light
The main light — it sets the pattern, the exposure, and the story of the portrait.
Fill light
The dimmer, larger, softer source that lifts the shadows the key casts.
Rim light
Light from behind and above that draws a bright edge and peels subject from background.
Hard light
From a small or distant source: crisp-edged shadows and high contrast.
Soft light
From a source that is large and close: shadows feather away; the flattering default.
Backlight 逆光
Light from behind the subject toward the camera — gyakkō — promising silhouettes and glow.
Color temperature
The warmth or coolness of light, in kelvin: candle about 1,800 K, daylight about 5,500 K.
White balance
The setting that renders the light's color as neutral; its mechanics are at white balance.
Golden hour
The first and last hour of sun — low, warm, and directional; the landscape's favorite.
Blue hour
The twilight after sunset (and before sunrise) when the sky itself glows deep blue.
High key
A bright image built mostly of light tones with few shadows — airy, even, clinical or gentle.
Low key
A dark image built mostly of shadows with a few highlights — moody, selective, theatrical.
Specular highlight
The tiny mirror-image of the light source itself — the catchlight in an eye, the gleam on chrome.

Color terms light, read by the mind

White balance
The kelvin-and-tint adjustment that neutralizes a light's cast; per photo in RAW, where it is free.
Gamut
The full set of colors a device can show or a space can describe — wider gamut, more colors.
Color space
The map defining which colors your numbers mean: sRGB everywhere, Adobe RGB for print, Display P3 newer.
Bit depth
Tonal steps per channel: 8-bit holds 256 levels; 16-bit holds vastly more room to edit.
Chromatic aberration
Colored fringes where a lens focuses different wavelengths unequally; usually one click to fix.
Vignetting
Darkening toward the frame's corners — an optical defect, a compositional aid, sometimes both.
Color cast
An unwanted overall tint, usually from mixed light; the white balance tool exists to evict it.
Hue
A color's position on the wheel — distinct from saturation (its purity) and lightness (its brightness).
Color grading
Deliberate stylization of color, often per tonal range; where photographs borrow the cinema look.
Monochrome 白黒
Rendering in tone without hue — shirokuro, "white-black" — the medium's oldest and newest look.

Output & process

The words of the last mile — files leaving the camera — and of the two centuries of chemistry and code that came before. The workflow itself is walked through at editing & workflow.

Output terms from file to page and screen

Export
Rendering a copy of a developed file at a chosen size, format, and color space; the master stays.
Resolution
Pixel dimensions — "higher resolution" means more pixels, not automatically sharper ones.
DPI / PPI
Dots per inch (printers) versus pixels per inch (screens); print wants about 300 ppi at final size.
Aspect ratio
Width to height of the frame — 3:2, 4:3, 1:1, 16:9 — the shape before any crop.
Crop
Trimming the frame after capture, to recompose or to fit a print's ratio.
Print sizes
Standard papers — 4×6, 8×10, A4 — each with its own ratio that crops must answer to.
Upscaling
Enlarging beyond native pixels by interpolation or AI inference; inventing, politely.
RatioWhere it comes from
3:235mm film and full-frame sensors; the 4×6 print's native shape
4:3Four Thirds, compacts, and most phones; the old television shape
1:1Square — medium-format 6×6 film, and the social feed's favorite
16:9Widescreen video and pano-style crops; screens, not paper
5:48×10 prints and large-format sheet film; the gallery portrait

Processes two centuries, oldest first

Daguerreotype
1839: a one-of-a-kind image on a polished silvered plate — sharp, mirror-like, unreproducible.
Collodion / wet plate
1850s: glass coated, exposed, and developed while still wet; the sharp workhorse of its era.
Film negative
Flexible roll coated in silver salts, dark where light struck, printed in reverse.
Slide film
Reversal (positive) film: what you shot is what you see — narrow latitude, projected straight.
Darkroom
The dim red room where film became negative and negative became print.
Developing 現像
Chemistry turning the invisible latent image into visible silver — genzō.
Stop bath
Mild acid that halts development on cue between developer and fixer.
Fixer 定着
Chemistry dissolving unexposed silver so the image stops reacting to light — it fixes it, permanently.
Enlarger
A vertical projector that throws the negative onto paper at a chosen scale.
Contact sheet
A whole roll printed small on one sheet — the editor's first pass and the era's paper trail.
Dodging & burning
Hand-shaping a print's exposure, lightening and darkening by area; every local slider descends from it.
Grain 粒子
The clumping of developed silver — noise's ancestor, and still a look people pay for.
RAW
The digital negative: near-unprocessed sensor data awaiting development. See RAW.
Demosaic
Computing full color from the sensor's one-color-per-pixel Bayer mosaic.
Stacking
Blending many frames: focus stacks for depth, exposure stacks for range, noise stacks for cleanliness.

Abbreviations the shorthand, decoded

AF
Autofocus.
AE
Automatic exposure; AE lock holds a metered value while you recompose.
AWB
Auto white balance — the camera's continuous guess at neutral color.
CA
Chromatic aberration; also the slider that removes it.
CFexpress
The current high-speed card standard in fast bodies, grown out of XQD.
DOF
Depth of field.
EV
Exposure value; also the scale exposure compensation is counted on.
EXIF
The metadata inside every file: camera, lens, settings, time, sometimes location.
GN
Guide number — a flash's power figure; GN divided by distance gives the f-number.
HDR
High dynamic range: blending bracketed frames into one wide-toned image.
HSS
High-speed sync: flash that works past the normal sync speed, paid for in power.
IBIS
In-body image stabilization.
IS / VR / OS
Image stabilization by maker: Canon, Nikon, Sigma — same idea, different letters.
ISO
The sensitivity setting, inherited from film speed ratings; higher, noisier.
JPEG
The universal compressed photo format — convenient, 8-bit, and lossy.
MFT
Micro Four Thirds: the 2×-crop mirrorless system with the shortest lenses.
MP
Megapixel — one million photosites, marketing's favorite integer.
ND
Neutral density: gray filter that cuts all light evenly, for slow shutters in sun.
RAW
Unprocessed sensor data; the digital negative.
RGB
Red, green, blue — how screens mix light; printers mix CMYK ink instead.
SLR
Single-lens reflex: one lens, a mirror, and a prism viewfinder.
TLR
Twin-lens reflex: one lens to view, one to take, used at waist level.
TTL
Through-the-lens: flash metered through the taking lens for automatic exposure.
WB
White balance.
APS-C
The roughly 1.5×-crop sensor size shared by most entry and mid-range bodies.
PASM
Program, aperture-priority, shutter-priority, manual — the four modes that matter.
